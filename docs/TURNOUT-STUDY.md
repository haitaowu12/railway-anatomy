# Turnout and crossing study

Follow a single wheelset, two wheels on one axle, through a complete turnout with a fixed crossing. The point-machine setting determines which route the wheelset takes. The study shows one axle. Bogie wheelbase and yaw are outside its scope.

## Two selected routes

| Point setting | Blades, viewed from the machine | Wheel at the crossing | Opposite checked wheel |
|---|---|---|---|
| A · straight | Near open, far closed | Far | Near |
| B · diverging | Near closed, far open | Near | Far |

The blades and both locks stay fixed while the wheelset passes. The two operating rods and two detector rods match the point-machine study. This independently driven schematic has no additional stretcher bar. The centre-line trace identifies the selected alignment; it is not a rail or a contact-force path.

The closed blade establishes the selected entry beside its stock rail. From the switch, the closure rails carry the selected route to the crossing.

Watch both wheels at the crossing. The wing rail and nose support the tread of the crossing wheel while its flange passes through the flangeway. Each route has its own opposite check rail. Across the axle, that rail can guide the back of the other wheel’s flange and keep the crossing wheel on the intended side of the nose. The drawing shows how the tread remains supported as it passes the flange gap.

## One geometry, two scales

**Whole turnout** and **Crossing close-up** use the same rails, crossing, check rails and wheelset. The close-up plays only the local crossing passage. Switching routes resets the wheelset to its approach. Links and browser history retain route, scale and playback position. The return to the point-machine study selects the corresponding physical endpoint.

The turnout nose and wing rails are separately selectable. A wheelset path drives the drawing, stage locations and readouts. The curved route uses normal offsets for the two wheel paths and geometric arc length for illustrative rotation. Wheel sections, rail profiles, curve, spacing and flangeways are dimensionless teaching proportions. The path is prescribed: there is no solution for wheel–rail contact forces, steering, differential rolling radii, suspension or vehicle dynamics.

The study uses a conventional fixed, tread-bearing common crossing with separate check rails. Other fixed, self-guarded, flange-bearing and movable crossing families need their own support and guidance explanations. A plain diamond provides two intersecting through routes. The fixed-diamond layout marks its two common and two obtuse crossings; that view has no wheel-contact animation. A crossover instead uses two turnouts and a connecting track.

## Checks

The geometry check follows both real wheel meshes through both routes and both scales. It probes rail-head support across each tread, checks clearance for the crossing-wheel flange and confirms an opposite check rail. The viewer check keeps both wheels inside the close-up camera. Navigation checks exercise return through a role and process stage to the originating route, close-up and playback position.
